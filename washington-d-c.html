Washington D.C.

Washington, D.C. puts your students face-to-face with our nation's past, present, and future. Established in 1790 as the capital of a brand-new country, the District today offers unmatched access to museums, monuments, and memorials that make classroom learning feel real. From the Declaration of Independence to time-honored traditions like the Changing of the Guard, D.C. gives students history they can walk through and touch.

Washington D.C. Landmarks

US Capitol

Construction on the Capitol began in 1793. For over a century it was the only building built for the use of the nation's legislature.

Lincoln Memorial

Standing at the west end of the National Mall, this neoclassical monument honors the 16th President and features his Gettysburg Address.

Washington Monument

Built between 1848 and 1884, the obelisk honors our first president and remains one of the tallest structures in the city.

Vietnam Veterans Memorial

This memorial honors members of the U.S. armed forces who died in service or remain unaccounted for during the Vietnam War.

World War II Memorial

Symbolic of the defining event of the 20th century, this memorial honors the spirit, sacrifice, and commitment of the American people.

Martin Luther King, Jr. Memorial

This memorial honors a man of courage, conviction, and hope, and his lasting impact on our nation's history.

Arlington National Cemetery

See the Changing of the Guard at the Tomb of the Unknowns and visit the grave sites of President John F. Kennedy, Sen. Robert F. Kennedy, and the Challenger crew.

Library of Congress

Started by Congress in 1800 with 740 books and 3 maps ordered from London as a reference collection, the library later grew into the largest in the world.

National Archives

Home to the Declaration of Independence, the Constitution, and the Bill of Rights, all on public display for students to see up close.

Monuments & Memorials

FDR Memorial

Dedicated May 2, 1997, this memorial spreads over 7.5 acres, tracing 12 years of history through four outdoor rooms, one for each of FDR's terms in office.

Jefferson Memorial

Modeled after the Pantheon of Rome, the Thomas Jefferson Memorial stands as America's foremost tribute to our third president.

Korean War Memorial

This memorial features 19 larger-than-life steel statues representing a squad on patrol, honoring those who served in the Korean War.
