Boston

Boston feels like a living textbook. Cobblestone streets, landmark meeting halls, and stories that shaped the nation sit around every corner. Alongside its Revolutionary roots, the city is known for its universities, its museums, and neighborhoods packed with character. From Boston Common and Faneuil Hall to Fenway Park, students get a trip that blends history, culture, and plenty of energy.

Highlights

Boston Common

The country's oldest public park has served as a cow pasture, a military camp, and the site of hangings, protest marches, and visits by dignitaries. Today it's the first stop on the Freedom Trail.

Bunker Hill Monument

Standing 221 feet tall, the monument marks the site of the first major battle of the American Revolution.

Freedom Trail

A 2.5 mile walking route linking 16 historic sites, from the Massachusetts State House to the USS Constitution.

Fenway Park

Home of the Red Sox since 1912, and one of the oldest ballparks still in use in the country.

Near Boston

Battlefields of Lexington & Concord

Visit the Minuteman Statue, the Old Burying Ground, and the National Historical Parks as students learn about the first battle of the Revolutionary War.

Walden Pond

Henry David Thoreau lived at Walden Pond from 1845 to 1847. His experience there produced the book Walden, credited with helping inspire awareness and respect for the natural world.
